# Mixtape Collective: project brief

_Originally written February 2026. Newer working rules live in `CLAUDE.md`._

## 1. What Mixtape Collective is

Mixtape Collective is a curated playlist project focused on intentional listening. It's not about maximizing followers or chasing algorithms, but about building coherent, well-paced playlists that feel like thoughtfully made mixtapes rather than infinite dumps of tracks.

Core idea:

- Playlists as objects (like tapes, records, zines)
- Strong internal logic, flow and sequencing
- Taste-driven, not trend-driven
- Designed to reward full listens, not shuffling

The project spans multiple genres but is unified by a shared curatorial philosophy.

## 2. Platforms and distribution

**Primary creation platform:** Deezer, chosen for catalog depth in niche genres and easier playlist management.

**Distribution**

- Soundiiz smart links for cross-platform sharing
- Spotify added later for reach and discoverability
- YouTube Music as a secondary endpoint
- SoundCloud explicitly dropped for now

**Key principle:** build once (on Deezer), distribute many. Avoid platform-specific optimizations that dilute curation quality.

## 3. Curatorial philosophy

**Flow over completeness**

- No playlist aims to be encyclopedic
- Redundancy is actively managed (artist caps, spacing rules)

**Structure matters.** Most playlists follow an implicit structure:

1. Hook / entry point: recognizable, high-impact tracks
2. Genre backbone: defining artists and sounds
3. Deep cuts: credibility and discovery
4. Breathing room: tempo or texture shifts
5. Exit: either a classic anchor or an intentional fade-out

**Rules we often enforce**

- No same artist too close together
- Limit artist repetition unless conceptually justified
- Avoid sudden energy cliffs unless intentional
- First and last ~5 tracks are sacred territory

## 4. Core genres and playlist canons

### Punk / hardcore / alternative

**Best of 80s Punk:** classic hardcore focus, tight runtime, no post-hardcore drift. Flow refined by minimal reordering, not track swaps.

**Rage Against Authority:** protest music across punk, metal and industrial. Starts with immediate impact, gradually widens stylistically. Modern political tracks allowed but must fit sonically.

**90s Grunge / Alt Rock:** carefully trimmed from larger lists. Artist duplication tightly controlled. Ending anchored by an undisputed classic. Avoids "adjacent but wrong" bands.

**College Radio Noise Rock:** Sonic Youth / Pixies / Pavement / Jesus Lizard axis. Balances accessibility and abrasion. Explicit spacing rules between same artists. Designed to feel like late-night college radio.

### Metal / heavy / math-adjacent

**Best of 00s Nu Metal:** era-correct, not ironic. MTV/TRL era framing. Flow optimized to avoid compression fatigue. Nostalgia with discipline.

**Math Metal / Angular Punk:** rhythm-forward, avoids prog excess, focus on tension and release.

### Psychedelic / electronic / ritual

**Psybient / Psychill:** Ott and Desert Dwellers closer to the core than Shpongle. Goa/psy lineage understood and respected. Long-form listening mindset. Tracks vetted for platform availability.

**Desert Psych / Ritual Rock:** drone, repetition, trance qualities. Tempo consistency matters more than genre labels.

### Emotional / post-rock / indie-math

**Emotive Math Rock:** published, light blue. Focus on melody, restraint and emotional arcs.

**Post-Rock / In-Between:** bridge between math rock and post-rock. Avoids bands already used in the emotive list. Preference for groove, texture and patience over crescendos.

## 5. Visual identity

**Core visual language:** worn cassette tapes, VHS / analog degradation, off-white aged labels, handwritten Sharpie text, slight rotation, imperfect framing.

**Philosophy:** imperfection signals care. Anti-algorithm aesthetics. Evokes physical mixtape culture.

Each playlist has a distinct title, a matching visual, and a short, casual description (no "AI tone", no academic language).

## 6. Tone and writing style

**Playlist descriptions:** casual, human, slightly opinionated. No em dashes. No marketing fluff. Sounds like a friend handing you a tape.

> These aren't the loudest songs. They're the ones that stay.

**Social posts:** short, confident, no hashtags unless they earn their place. Focus on vibe, not genre taxonomies.

## 7. Ongoing constraints and decisions

- Avoid over-representation of favorite bands
- Willing to drop great songs if they hurt flow
- Prefer fewer, stronger playlists over constant releases
- Canon over content churn

## 8. Still refining

- Long-term canon structure (how playlists relate to each other)
- Visual consistency across platforms
- Discoverability without compromising taste
- Whether to split certain playlists into "Essentials" and "Expanded" versions

## 9. What success looks like

Not huge follower counts or viral playlists, but:

- People listening start to finish
- People saving multiple playlists
- A recognizable curatorial fingerprint
- Mixtape Collective feeling like a place, not a feed
